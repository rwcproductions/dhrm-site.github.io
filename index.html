What if your clinic’s lobby held a third kind of license?

Picture a clinic where an MD, a DO, and a DHRM practice down the hall from one another. The third is a Doctor of Hygienic-Restorative Medicine: a fully trained physician from a branch of medicine that never took shape, but plausibly could have.

An old calling, carried forward

Seventh-day Adventists were entrusted with a healing work: care for body and soul together, teaching people how to live, service to the poor, and intelligent cooperation with the laws God built into the body. In real history, that work was partly narrowed, resisted, and pulled apart.

This site asks what it might have become if it had stayed faithful to its purpose and matured into a recognized branch of medicine, the way osteopathy organized itself into a profession. In that alternate history, the branch is called Hygienic-Restorative Medicine, and its practitioners are restorative physicians.

What the name means

  • Hygienic

    The older sense of the word: the ordered conditions of life. Air, water, food, rest, exercise, sunlight, cleanliness, and temperance, treated as medical facts rather than afterthoughts.

  • Restorative

    The aim. Not only relief of symptoms, but recovery of function, order, and wholeness where that is possible, and faithful care where it is not.

  • Physician

    Trained in anatomy, pathology, diagnosis, surgery, drugs, and emergency care. Not an alternative to medicine, but a branch of it with a different center.

Where to start

A thought experiment. Restorative physicians do not exist, and no one has ever earned a DHRM. The imagined profession is a mirror, not a plan or a prediction. It helps show what was entrusted, what was lost along the way, and what faithfulness might still ask of us.

The approach page explains the idea in more depth. The visit page shows what it might have looked like in an exam room, and where its limits would be.
